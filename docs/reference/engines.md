# Engines

The LLM, speech, transcription and depth engines load at startup and stay in memory, since a conversation uses them at once. The rest load the first time a request needs them, which adds up to a few seconds to that request, and unload after 10 minutes unused: `LITE_ASSISTANT_IDLE_UNLOAD` sets the seconds, and `0` keeps them loaded. `/v1/models` says whether each of those is `loaded`.

- **LiteRT-LM (LLM):** Google's Gemma 4. It tries the GPU first (Gemma 4 E4B with speculative decoding) and falls back to the CPU (Gemma 4 E2B). The model loads once at startup and stays in memory.
  - `LITERTLM_BACKEND=gpu` or `cpu` forces a backend.
  - `LITERTLM_MODEL=e2b` or `e4b` chooses the model, whichever backend loads. E4B on the CPU is smarter but decodes at about half the speed.
  - `LITERTLM_THREADS` sets the CPU thread count. 2–3 decode fastest on Windows; LiteRT-LM's default is fastest on Linux.
  - `LITERTLM_MAX_TOKENS` sets the context length (default 4096).
  - A request can switch to any other downloaded LiteRT-LM model, such as the other Gemma or Qwen3; see [Switching models](../guides/switch-models.md).
- **Kokoro (TTS):** on-device speech, and the default voice, in American and British English, Spanish, French, Hindi, Italian, Japanese, Brazilian Portuguese and Mandarin. Each voice's text is read by its own language's rules: `ff_siwis` reads French as French.
- **EmbeddingGemma 2 (embeddings):** Google's 740M multimodal embedding model, on LiteRT-LM's CPU backend. It was trained with task prefixes on text, which improve results: `task: search result | query: {query}` for a search query and `title: none | text: {document}` for what it searches, or `task: sentence similarity | query: {text}` on both sides of a comparison. Images and audio take no prefix.
- **Moonshine (STT):** Useful Sensors' speech recognition, on ONNX Runtime. It takes clips of 0.1 to 64 seconds.
- **Whisper large-v3-turbo (STT):** OpenAI's speech recognition, int8, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/whisper-large-v3-turbo) (MIT, 1.1 GB). Slower than Moonshine, but more accurate, and it hears about 100 languages. It takes clips up to 2 minutes, 30 s at a time. It loads on first use and unloads when idle.
- **MoGe-2 (depth):** Microsoft's monocular geometry model, ViT-S, as [Google's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/MoGe-2-LiteRT) (MIT). From one photo it estimates each point's distance in meters and the camera's field of view, in about half a second on the CPU. It uses about 700 MB of memory.
- **YOLOX (object detection):** Megvii's YOLOX-Tiny, as [Google's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/yolox-tiny-litert) (Apache-2.0, 10 MB). It finds COCO's 80 kinds of object, such as people, chairs, cups, dogs and cars, in about 60 ms on the CPU. `YOLOX_MODEL=nano` picks the 2 MB version, which is faster and misses more.
- **YuNet (faces):** ShiqiYu's libfacedetection model, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/YuNet-Face-LiteRT) (BSD-3-Clause, 0.3 MB). It finds faces, with their eyes, nose and mouth corners, in about 15 ms on the CPU.
- **pyannote 3.1 (speakers):** pyannote's segmentation-3.0 (MIT, 5.7 MB, on onnxruntime) and WeSpeaker's ResNet34 (CC-BY-4.0 weights, 13 MB), as [litert-community's conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/Speaker-Diarization-LiteRT). The first finds up to three voices in 10 s windows, the second embeds 5 s of a voice, and they're clustered into who spoke when, as pyannote 3.1 does.
- **CMGAN (noise removal):** Ruizhe Cao's conformer, trained on VoiceBank-DEMAND, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/CMGAN-LiteRT) (MIT, 4 MB). It cleans speech 2 s at a time; XNNPACK declines its fp16 graph, so it runs at about real time on the CPU.
- **CREPE (pitch):** Kim, Salamon, Li and Bello's pitch tracker, the full model, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/CREPE-pitch-LiteRT) (MIT, 45 MB). The pitch of 64 ms of sound, from about 32 Hz to 2 kHz, every 50 ms, in about 20 ms each on the CPU.
- **Qwen3-Reranker (reranking):** Qwen's Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B-LiteRT) (Apache-2.0, 1.2 GB). It reads a query and a document together, up to 256 tokens of both, and says how likely the document answers it, in about 0.4 s a document on the CPU. Its fp16 weights take about 2.7 GB of memory as fp32, so it loads on first use and unloads when idle.
- **PANNs (sounds):** Kong et al.'s CNN14, trained on Google's AudioSet, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/PANNs-CNN14-AudioSet-LiteRT) (CC-BY-4.0, 162 MB). It names 527 kinds of sound, from speech and music to a doorbell, a dog, breaking glass or a smoke alarm, in about 0.1 s per 10 s of audio on the CPU.
- **GLiClass (text classification):** Knowledgator's GLiClass-Edge v3.0, a 33M ModernBERT, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/GLiClass-Edge-v3.0-LiteRT) (Apache-2.0, 150 MB). It scores up to 25 labels, any you name, against an English text in about 20 ms on the CPU.
- **CPGA-Net (image editing):** Shyandram's low-light enhancer, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/CPGA-Net-LowLight-LiteRT) (MIT, 0.1 MB). It brightens a dark photo; it sees 256x256, and the brightening it does there is scaled up to the full photo.
- **NIMA (image quality):** idealo's NIMA MobileNets, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/NIMA-LiteRT) (Apache-2.0, 13 MB). They score a photo's technical and aesthetic quality in about 20 ms each.
- **PP-OCRv6 (reading text):** PaddleOCR's small detector and recognizers, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/PP-OCRv6-Small-LiteRT) (Apache-2.0, 75 MB). They read printed text in 85 languages and scripts, Latin, Chinese and Japanese among them, in about a quarter of a second for a photo with a few lines.
- **Line finder (lines):** no model: a line is what stands out from the floor in each band across the photo, followed up from the bottom. About 7 ms for a 640x480 photo.
- **Ultra-Fast-Lane-Detection (lanes):** cfzd's ResNet18, trained on CULane's Chinese roads, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/Ultra-Fast-Lane-Detection-LiteRT) (MIT, 178 MB). Up to four lane markings from a dashcam's view, in about 60 ms on the CPU.
- **MoViNet-A0 Stream (actions):** Google's streaming video model, trained on Kinetics-600's 600 actions, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/MoViNet-A0-Stream-LiteRT) (Apache-2.0, 15 MB). It takes a frame at a time, remembering those before, in about 5 ms a frame.
- **CLIPSeg (segmentation):** CIDAS's CLIPSeg rd64 refined, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/CLIPSeg-rd64-LiteRT) (Apache-2.0, 277 MB). It finds anything named, such as "the cup" or "the grass", as a mask, in about 0.6 s for the photo and 50 ms more for each name.
- **HSEmotion (faces):** Savchenko's EfficientNet-B0, trained on AffectNet, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/HSEmotion-B0-LiteRT) (Apache-2.0, 8 MB). It reads a face's expression, one of eight, in about 10 ms.
- **L2CS-Net (faces):** Abdelrahman et al.'s gaze model, trained on Gaze360, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/L2CS-Gaze360-LiteRT) (MIT, 48 MB). It tells where a face is looking, in about 0.1 s.
- **RTMPose (poses):** OpenMMLab's RTMPose-s for bodies and RTMPose for hands, as litert-community's LiteRT conversions ([body](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/RTMPose-s-LiteRT), [hand](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/RTMPose-Hand-LiteRT); Apache-2.0, 39 MB). They find a person's 17 joints and a hand's 21, in about 10 ms each.
- **ormbg (image editing):** schirrmacher's background remover, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/ormbg-LiteRT) (Apache-2.0, 169 MB). It sees 1024x1024, and its matte, scaled back up, becomes the photo's transparency.
- **MI-GAN (image editing):** Picsart's inpainter, trained on Places2, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/MI-GAN-512-Places2-LiteRT) (MIT, 16 MB). It erases what a mask covers and fills it in from around it; CLIPSeg makes the mask from a name.
- **Real-ESRGAN (image editing):** xinntao's general x4v3 upscaler, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/real-esrgan-x4v3-litert) (BSD-3-Clause, 3.4 MB). It makes a photo 4 times as wide and tall, in overlapping 128-pixel tiles.
- **PlantNet-300K (plants):** cpoisson's ResNet18, trained on Pl@ntNet-300K, as [litert-community's LiteRT conversion](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/PlantNet-300K-ResNet18-LiteRT) (Apache-2.0, 47 MB). It names a plant's species, one of 1081, mostly of Europe, in about 30 ms.
